# Paper Reading

Turn a paper into a clear, accessible, deep, and detailed explanation with rich visualization and meaningful interaction. **Read and apply the user's [13-part prompt](references/user-prompt.md) for every DEEP READ.** Its first eight questions establish understanding; questions 9–13 extend that understanding into scrutiny, reproduction, counterexamples, subsequent research, and a new research hypothesis. All 13 are default obligations, not optional lenses. Preserve the publication-time background and evidence/rendering safeguards below. Explicit user requests for a shorter scope or another format take precedence.

**Narrative first, visualization second.** The prose must explain why the problem matters, why existing knowledge leaves a gap, how a plausible idea follows, how it works, and what evidence establishes. A polished page, a module inventory, or a chain of cards is not a substitute for that explanatory argument. Write and check the prose spine before choosing visuals; do not shorten essential explanation to fit a layout.

**Make consequential method changes unmistakable.** For every DEEP READ, read and apply [method-salience](references/method-salience.md). Explicitly highlight important methods the paper introduces, modifies, or adopts, including non-headline mechanisms that change computation or information flow. Being mentioned once in an overview does not count as being explained. Give the selected mechanisms descriptive, reader-language subheadings in the visible method body, explain their operation and role in the running example, and show them in the relevant diagram. Distinguish new-to-this-system changes from borrowed ideas and verified original contributions; do not call everything the authors use novel. This strengthens questions 4–7 without adding a fourteenth question or using the eventual method as a premise in the pre-paper idea reconstruction.

## Choose depth and lens

- **Default: DEEP READ.** “Read this paper,” “仔细读,” and requests for a mind map or diagram mean a complete read. Do not infer a critique-first objective or stop after a preliminary relevance judgment.
- **SCAN** only when the user asks for triage, a quick overview, or whether to read further. Stop after the overview only in this mode.
- **REVIEW lens** when the user asks whether conclusions hold, for weaknesses, or for a reviewer-style assessment. Expand the default assumption/counterexample analysis with the [critique matrix](references/critique-matrix.md) after explaining the paper.
- **RESEARCH lens** when the user supplies an active research question or asks for comparison, reproduction, or next experiments. Tailor the mandatory questions 9–13 to that context; optionally add the [research card](references/research-card.md) and [evidence state](references/evidence-state.md). Do not invent a user research agenda.

Read [reading modes](references/reading-modes.md) only when depth or lens is ambiguous. Select the relevant [paper-type protocol](references/paper-types.md) for a theory, benchmark, dataset, survey, empirical, or systems paper.

## Build the reading atlas

Read the paper in passes: inspect the abstract, introduction, overview figures, conclusions, and main results for a provisional map; then read methods, experiments, captions, appendices, and definitions to correct and deepen that map. A provisional map is never the final answer in DEEP READ. Confirm the paper identity, version, and access to the sections needed for its central claims before promising a complete read. If only an abstract or truncated source is available, obtain the full source or tell the user what is missing; do not present a partial reading as a complete atlas.

Prefer the supplied/local PDF. When it is missing, incomplete, or needs background verification, retrieve the matching full text and primary sources through arXiv, proceedings, publishers, or official author releases as available. Verify identity and revision; web summaries are discovery aids, not replacements for the paper. Record inaccessible sources and never imply a search or full-text check happened when it did not.

Before drafting the method, **read and apply [historical-context](references/historical-context.md)**. Research background is required for every DEEP READ, not an optional REVIEW/RESEARCH lens. Establish the first-publication horizon separately from the local PDF revision date; reconstruct what relevant prior work had achieved, what the field still could not do, and why the paper was proposed then. Start from the paper's introduction, related work, and references. Verify pivotal predecessor contributions or uncertain dates against primary sources; describe unverified historical priority as unknown. Do not use a later revision's citations or today's leaderboard as evidence of what was known at first publication. Explain the task-specific prerequisite concepts a reader needs, not a generic field history.

For every DEEP READ, **read and apply [narrative-logic](references/narrative-logic.md) and [research-extension](references/research-extension.md)** before drafting. Build the following 13-part explanatory spine inside **one continuous reading document**. The short global orientation comes first. Questions 1–2 share the first navigable background chapter to preserve the historical-context contract. These are logical obligations, not 13 compulsory equal-length boxes; adapt the method, mathematics, and evidence to the paper type.

Use this explanation order by default, rather than copying the paper's section order or a visual template. If process chronology differs, make diagrams convey the actual process. Explain unfamiliar concepts before relying on them, connect chapters with the unresolved question that motivates the next step, and place cross-cutting components with the operations they support. Do not add a default section discussing the document's chapter order; show the paper's substance directly. Keep navigation labels short enough to scan.

**Brief global orientation:** give a short paper-specific paragraph and a labeled concept map locating the problem, idea, contribution, and evidence. This is a preview, not a substitute for the explanatory body or a premise in the idea reconstruction.

1. **Problem and value:** in the first navigable publication-time background chapter, define the actual research question, task setting, inputs/desired outcomes, constraints, and success criterion. Explain why the unresolved problem matters and what capability or practical/scientific value solving it would enable. Separate hoped-for value from demonstrated benefit.
2. **Prior solutions and remaining gap:** in that same background chapter, state the first-publication boundary and field maturity, explain prerequisite concepts, and trace approximately 3–6 relevant predecessors with their actual contribution, remaining limitation, relation to this problem, and source. Answer whether the problem was unsolved, partially solved, or solved only under different conditions; do not dismiss all prior work as failures. Close on the precise unresolved bottleneck. Concurrent work is not automatically an influence; later work must be labeled. Use the audit markers and checks in [historical-context](references/historical-context.md).
3. **Evidence-grounded idea reconstruction, before the method:** build a plausible explanatory bridge from pre-paper knowledge, documented failure modes, observations, and prior work to a design hypothesis. Do not use this paper's new modules, objective, claimed contribution, or successful results as premises. Mark this as a pedagogical reconstruction, not the authors' private thought process or verified discovery history; attribute an inspiration to the authors only with an explicit source. State uncertainty instead of inventing a story.
4. **Core intuition:** explain the essence of the actual idea in a few plain-language sentences: what changes relative to the old approach, why it could address the bottleneck, and under what assumption. Then map it to the paper's terminology. A slogan, contribution list, or analogy alone is insufficient; distinguish intuition from proof or experimental fact.
5. **Concrete method and running example:** explain the full input → processing → output pipeline in numbered steps. Prefer one real paper example or a verifiable task instance and trace the same instance through the consequential stages; explicitly label a constructed example and any schematic, uncomputed, or unavailable intermediates. Keep training, inference, and evaluation distinct. Unpack the essential mechanisms beside the steps they support: definitions, data movement, decisions, feedback, and each central choice's constraint → mechanism → cost/assumption. **Explicitly surface the selected new, modified, and noteworthy adopted methods**, using a brief old→changed→effect orientation after the core intuition and dedicated explanatory subheadings where they operate. For each explain what it is, provenance, why it is used, inputs/state/outputs, when it is learned or applied, cost, and the evidence or missing ablation; preserve meaningful counts such as “64 learnable virtual rows (thinking rows).” Do not bury such mechanisms inside “architecture improvements” or an English-only name list. Do not invent an execution trace or claim to have run the method.
6. **Accessible mathematics or theoretical argument:** when present, teach only the necessary foundations, define notation before use, and walk from the starting assumptions/problem to the central objective, derivation, or proof in justified steps. Explain each step's mathematical operation and intuitive meaning; connect the result back to the method and example. Separate the paper's derivation from an added pedagogical derivation. If no theoretical derivation exists, say so briefly and skip it; still explain formulas essential to the implementation or metric. Never manufacture a theorem for an empirical heuristic. Use renderer-compatible `$…$` and `$$…$$` mathematics, not code fences.
7. **Question-driven experiments:** organize the central evidence as **question → experiment designed to answer it → answer and scope**. Explain the comparator/control, task/protocol, and what result would distinguish the proposed explanation. Lead with the core experimental logic, not a leaderboard dump. Keep only the decisive numbers in the main narrative, with their actual units, denominators, conditions, and source; retain a compact data table for quantitative charts. Distinguish whole-system comparisons from controlled ablations, observations from interpretations, and a supported claim from one left untested. Adapt to proofs or coverage evidence for non-empirical papers.
8. **Takeaways:** synthesize a small set of specific lessons connecting the problem, reusable insight, mechanism, and evidence before the research extension. State what changed in our understanding, when the approach is useful, and what remains conditional or unresolved. These are grounded conclusions, not a repeated abstract, generic praise, a repeated figure list, or commentary about the reading document. Keep minor source discrepancies near the affected fact.
9. **Most fragile assumption:** identify the consequential assumption most likely to fail, its role in the mechanism or inference, evidence for its fragility, and the observable consequence of failure. Distinguish an author-acknowledged limitation from your diagnosis; uncertainty is preferable to an unsupported ranking.
10. **One-week minimal reproduction:** choose one narrow, consequential claim that can realistically be checked in seven days. Specify data, code/model access, hardware/time budget, baseline/control, metric, acceptance criterion, schedule, and a fallback. This is a proposed experiment, not a claim of execution or a promise to reproduce the entire paper.
11. **Counterexample:** propose a fair, concrete case or controlled distribution that challenges a named claim or assumption. Explain the expected failure mechanism, comparator, what result would undermine the claim, and what result would count against your objection. Separate an in-scope falsification from an out-of-scope stress test. Never invent a successful refutation.
12. **Subsequent research, using search tools:** actually search for extensions, replications, criticisms, negative results, and changed understanding up to the search date. Verify relevant primary sources and their relationship to this paper; report dates, versions, evidence, and limits. Record queries and access gaps in the audit. A citation is not proof of an extension or rebuttal. No verified follow-up found is a valid finding, not proof that none exists. Keep this retrospective section separate from publication-time premises.
13. **A substantive follow-up idea:** derive one valuable research direction from a concrete limitation and unmet need, informed by the subsequent-literature search. Explain the new question or formulation, proposed mechanism, closest work and difference, falsifiable prediction, minimal discriminating experiment, failure criterion, and risks. Aim for a conceptual contribution rather than swapping components or scaling a model; label novelty as provisional within the searched scope, never guaranteed.

## Writing and information discipline

- Use a natural, technically precise teaching voice, taking the user's Karpathy/Kaiming He reference as a request for concrete intuition, disciplined reasoning, and readable explanations, not impersonation or fabricated quotations. Each sentence should add a claim, definition, example, causal link, condition, or useful qualification.
- Prefer flowing prose and explicit causal logic. Avoid generic praise, filler, excessive dashes or quotation marks, and stock contrasts such as “不是……而是……”. Be concise about the essence, detailed about the mechanism and reasoning.
- **Strictly distinguish four information classes near the affected statement:** **论文原文 / Paper claim** (including explicitly identified reported observations), **已有文献 / Prior-literature finding**, **合理推断 / Evidence-based inference**, and **不确定猜测 / Uncertain conjecture**. Use clear local attribution or labels without mechanically tagging every sentence. Give primary-source anchors for the first two, premises and reasoning for the third, and uncertainty plus a validation route for the fourth. A paper's claim is not automatically an established fact; a new proposal is not an observed result. Preserve these distinctions in charts and interactive views as well as prose.

For every diagram, use [visual-reading](references/visual-reading.md): one question per view, labeled relationships, selective detail, and source anchors. A diagram gives conceptual orientation; it does not replace explanations, equations, tables, or original figures. Do not flatten the paper into one giant graph or force a method flow onto a theory or survey paper.

Extract and simplify the paper's core visual explanations into the HTML: a paper-specific overview, a worked mechanism/pipeline or proof view, and question-driven evidence views as appropriate. Readable original figure excerpts and faithful explanatory redraws should complement one another. **The default deliverable includes meaningful offline interaction**, such as tracing the running example, comparing a baseline with the proposed mechanism, or selecting an experimental question to inspect its evidence. Name the reader action and insight before choosing controls; a citation toggle or decorative animation alone does not satisfy this requirement. Use static views wherever they communicate better, without dropping the document-level interaction requirement. Do not invent simulated results to make an interactive demo. Record the design rationale in the Markdown audit source; see [visual-reading](references/visual-reading.md).

## Default deliverable

The publication-time background must be visible in the same HTML and present in its chapter navigation, not hidden in citations or placed only in a separate report. It must explain earlier contributions and the unresolved problem before the main method. Keep first-publication context separate from any explicitly labeled later-revision or hindsight note.

For DEEP READ, deliver **one self-contained HTML file** that a reader can follow from overview to details without opening separate diagram pages or returning to the paper for essential explanations. Put the overview, mechanism diagrams, readable original figure excerpts when useful, formulas, experiment data, worked examples, explanations, and source pointers in the same document. A source pointer is for verification, never the entire content of a card or section; hide PDF page and figure citations in the default HTML reading view and provide an explicit way to reveal them. The audit Markdown retains all references. Show the actual setup, baseline, observation, and interpretation beside each consequential experiment. Embed locally generated graphics and any Archify views into that file; separate JSON, Markdown, and diagram HTML are editable build inputs, not the primary reader-facing output. Keep a Markdown audit source alongside the HTML. For SCAN or when the user explicitly requests another format, match that request.

A reusable renderer for this repository's examples is [render-reading.cjs](scripts/render-reading.cjs); use it when its Markdown/visual inputs fit the paper, or create an equivalent single-file HTML. Its build uses this repository's files and `package.json` dependencies only. Do not require a separately installed local skill, Archify CLI, or a machine-specific path. Pre-rendered interactive HTML may be embedded as an optional input. Do not turn the example's layout into a fixed template for every paper type. Before the first render in a fresh checkout, run `npm ci` in the skill directory (the renderer needs `marked` and `katex`) and optionally `npm run doctor` as a smoke build. Follow [renderer-format](references/renderer-format.md) for the exact input contract — fenced visual blocks and their JSON fields, citation patterns that hide with the source toggle, image formats and paths, math syntax, and the build errors that guard them. For an ordinary reading, write the deliverable into a dedicated directory of the user's own project (for example `paper-readings/<slug>/`), with the audit source and its `assets/` co-located; any output path is fine. Invoke the renderer from the installed skill path (`node <skill-dir>/scripts/render-reading.cjs <source>.md <output>.html`), which works with sources and outputs outside the skill directory. The `examples/<slug>-deep-read.md` + `docs/<slug>-deep-read.html` convention applies only when contributing to this repository's public gallery; [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) lists how those are registered.

Choose the reading language before drafting: follow an explicit user preference; otherwise use the language of the user's request, and use English when the request gives no language cue. The paper's own language does not override the reader's language. If a mixed-language request leaves the intended output genuinely unclear, ask once. Keep headings, diagrams, captions, alt text, tables, and navigation in the chosen language; retain necessary technical names and original quotations. Declare that choice on the first line of every Markdown audit source with `<!-- paper-reading-lang: en -->` or `<!-- paper-reading-lang: zh-CN -->`. The bundled renderer requires this marker and sets the HTML `lang` and interface labels from it. For another requested language, use an equivalent HTML build with the correct `lang` and localized interface instead of silently switching languages. Name the output language in the delivery message and label the language of published examples.

Choose a paper-appropriate visual theme without changing the evidence or reading structure. The bundled renderer offers `forest`, `cobalt`, `plum`, and `saffron`; add an optional second source line such as `<!-- paper-reading-theme: cobalt -->`. It uses `forest` when that line is absent. The choice is editorial, not automatic content detection. Keep heading, card, and diagram titles free of terminal sentence punctuation in both languages unless punctuation is part of a quoted title or notation.

## Evidence and rendering contract

- Before rendering a DEEP READ, run `node <skill-dir>/scripts/check-background.cjs <source>.md` and repair missing background structure. This checker verifies presence and placement, **not** chronology, priority, or scientific truth; manually apply the historical-context quality gate as well. Existing upstream gallery examples are not retroactively treated as proof of compliance with this project's local extension.
- Tie important facts, nodes, arrows, formulas, and numbers to exact PDF page/section/figure/table/appendix references in the audit source. Keep those references retrievable in the HTML without showing them by default. In a PDF, verify captions and table cells visually when extraction is uncertain; when chart labels or bar-to-category mappings are ambiguous in the text layer, use the coordinate verification in [figure-extraction](references/figure-extraction.md) and record conflicting readings instead of guessing. Record source version and conflicting numbers instead of silently resolving them.
- If prose invokes an equation to explain a mechanism, render the full relevant equation beside that explanation, preserve its denominator and masks, and define the variables readers need. Distinguish an algebraically equivalent screen layout from a different objective. If a chart uses values from a paper figure, visibly label it as a redraw or approximation and name the source panel; visibly label embedded original figures as original. Figure identifiers remain readable even when page-number references are hidden.
- Introduce every embedded original figure in visible prose before the image: say what question it answers, which panel, axis, or arrow to inspect, and what the reader should learn from it. The figure caption may add provenance and a short reminder, but must not be the only explanation. Crop figure regions from the PDF with the recipe in [figure-extraction](references/figure-extraction.md), keeping the paper's figure number in the filename. Check all embedded figures for orphaned images after rendering, including figures referenced only inside hidden PDF citations.
- Apply the four information classes above throughout, and identify reported observations within paper/literature claims. End-to-end comparisons describe system results; controlled ablations can isolate a mechanism. Proposed reproduction, counterexamples, and follow-up ideas must remain visibly unexecuted unless actual execution is documented.
- Use a readable Markdown source as the portable audit copy. Derive HTML content from that source and structured figure data; keep figures, numbers, equations, and qualifiers aligned. Important content must remain readable without scripts and offline. Render math as math, not code text.
- Give the first screen paper-specific orientation rather than decorative shapes or generic slogans. For before/after results, show both values on a shared scale, label the metric and unit, and keep the numerical table. Render inline formulas as part of their sentences, with following punctuation in the same inline unit; reserve separate blocks for display formulas. Bundle the math stylesheet and fonts into offline HTML, and retain accessible math semantics. Inspect the final HTML for broken Markdown markers, isolated inline formulas, overflow, and figures whose caption only points elsewhere.
- An editorial overview graphic can borrow the clear hierarchy of the project homepage preview: a paper-specific question, a compact mechanism view, and an evidence view in a shared visual language. Replace its content with paper-specific relationships and verified numbers. Static graphics must not imitate chapter navigation or clickable controls. Label illustrative paths and schematic bars as such; never let a decorative bar imply a measured result. Introduce the view in prose and explain the important arrows and quantities nearby.
- Embed the required working interaction inside the final HTML as a supplementary exploration view. Use Archify if it fits and is available, or build the view with self-contained HTML/SVG and local script when needed; the reading skill must not depend on Archify or another installed skill. Keep the main path, every consequential branch, and its learning signal or result readable in visible prose or static graphics without scripts. Preserve source inputs and links to paper evidence. See [visual-reading](references/visual-reading.md).
- Before delivery, apply the [reading quality gates](evals/reading-quality.md), the prose-only logic check in [narrative-logic](references/narrative-logic.md), and the mechanism coverage/visibility audit in [method-salience](references/method-salience.md) to the source, Markdown audit copy, and final HTML. Repair failed gates before presenting a DEEP READ. A selected key mechanism that is only named in the hero, hidden in a disclosure, or missing from its pipeline view fails the visibility audit. Headings and render checks cannot establish narrative coherence. The public [reader tasks](evals/reader-tasks.md) illustrate how to test whether a person can reconstruct a paper using only the HTML; passing a renderer test is not evidence of reader comprehension.
- The response should let a reader answer all 13 questions: understand the problem, prior gap, plausible idea route, intuition, worked method, mathematics, experiments, and takeaways; then identify the fragile assumption, plan a one-week test, formulate a fair counterexample, explain verified subsequent research, and evaluate a substantive but provisionally novel follow-up idea. Explicitly report missing source access, unavailable search, or unresolved novelty checks instead of presenting incomplete work as fully verified.
